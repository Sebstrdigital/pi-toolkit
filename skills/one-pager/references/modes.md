# Presentation modes

Use these as structural guidance, not fixed page layouts. Read all selected modes and weave them together.

## Briefing

For research findings, comparisons, recommendations, and status reports.

Lead with the question and the answer supported by evidence. Follow with key findings, a useful comparison table, counterevidence/limitations, and a recommendation or next steps where warranted. Place methodology and source detail in expandable sections. Avoid making a recommendation stronger than the evidence.

## Guide

For instructions, onboarding, tutorials, and practical procedures.

Lead with the intended outcome and prerequisites. Provide ordered steps, examples, checks for success, and troubleshooting. Use a process diagram for meaningful branches. Surface risky or irreversible actions before their steps. Commands are illustrative instructions, not permission for this skill to execute them.

## Explainer

For learning, conceptual synthesis, architecture, and understanding relationships.

Lead with the mental model. Build from familiar concepts to unfamiliar ones with examples, flow/relationship diagrams, and plain-language explanations. Provide text equivalents for visuals. Put deeper mechanics in expandable details. State where an analogy or inferred relationship is explanatory rather than a source fact. Attribute evidence from videos/transcripts and research honestly.

## Decision review

For ADRs, proposals, agreements, and other material requiring a position or feedback.

Lead with what accepting the proposal actually means—and what it does not authorize. Present scope/non-goals, proposed choices, rationale, alternatives, risks, and later acceptance gates. Attach review controls only to real current proposals. Keep settled decisions and deliberately deferred details clearly labelled. Include the full authoritative source as a local document, not just a summary. Browser positions and exported feedback are drafts until explicitly submitted under the project's process.

## Combine modes

Choose two or more modes based on the user's actual selection. Establish one main narrative and layer complementary elements into it:

- Explainer + Decision review: explain how the proposal works, then connect the architecture and trade-offs to reviewable decisions.
- Briefing + Explainer: summarize findings, then explain the concepts needed to understand them.
- Explainer + Guide: build the mental model, then provide a practical walkthrough.
- Briefing + Decision review: summarize the evidence, then present the proposals it supports and the user's feedback fields.

Do not duplicate overview, sources, or conclusion per mode. Do not add review controls unless Decision review is selected. Avoid forcing all four modes onto a simple topic.
